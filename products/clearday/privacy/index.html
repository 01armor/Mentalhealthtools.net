This policy explains what Clearday collects, why, who else handles it, how long we keep it, and how you can see it, change it or delete it. Clearday is made by Mental Health Tools LLC ("we" or "us"). Some of what you share in Clearday is about your health, so we've written this in plain language and tried to be exact about what happens to it.

The short version

  • We collect what you put into Clearday, plus a few things your phone tells the app, such as your time zone.
  • Reflections uses AI from Anthropic. If you allow it, our server sends Anthropic what you write in Reflections, your first name, some of your progress and short excerpts from your check-in notes, so it can write replies. Anthropic doesn't use it to train its AI.
  • We never sell your information. We don't show ads, and the app has no tracking or analytics tools.
  • Clearday never uploads your profile photo. It stays on your phone.
  • You can turn Reflections off or delete your account yourself, any time, in the app. You can also start over (once a day).
  • Clearday is a self-help tool, not health care or a crisis service. If you're in crisis, call or text 988. In an emergency, call 911.

Who we are and what this policy covers

Clearday is made by Mental Health Tools LLC, a limited liability company in New Jersey, United States. We decide how your information is used, so we're the company responsible for it (privacy laws call this the "controller").

This policy covers the Clearday app for iPhone and the Clearday pages at www.clearday.pro. Our company website, mentalhealthtools.net, has its own short website privacy page, summarized in Our websites below.

Clearday is offered in the United States only.

When you create an account, you agree to the Terms of Use and this Privacy Policy. Reflections, the app's AI feature, asks for your separate permission before it shares anything with its AI provider.

Clearday is a self-help tool, not health care or a crisis service

Clearday is a self-help and self-reflection tool for anyone who wants to cut back on or quit cannabis, alcohol, nicotine, vaping or stimulants. It is not therapy, treatment, counseling or medical care, and we are not a health care provider.

Reflections is an AI, not a person, and it can get things wrong. Don't rely on facts it states without checking them, and don't rely on it for medical advice. Talk to a doctor or another qualified professional before making decisions about your health.

Clearday is not an emergency or crisis service. Nobody watches what you write as it happens. If you're thinking about harming yourself or you're in danger, call or text 988, or chat at 988lifeline.org (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline). In an emergency, call 911.

Even though Clearday isn't health care, we treat what you share as sensitive health information throughout this policy.

What we collect and how we get it

We get information in four ways: you type it in, the app creates it as you use Clearday, your phone sends it, or Apple sends it. We also get whatever you send us when you contact us.

What you type in

  • Your account. Your email address and a password. We store your password only in scrambled (hashed) form that can't be turned back into the password, so we never keep the password itself.
  • Your name. When you sign up, the app fills in a first name guessed from your email address (for example, jane.doe@example.com becomes "Jane"). You can change it in You › Privacy & data. It's used in the app and in Reflections. Our confirmation email greets you with a name guessed from your email address, not the name you set.
  • Your plan. The substances you're working on (cannabis, alcohol, nicotine, vaping or stimulants), a weekly spending estimate for each, your day one, the reasons you pick, and up to 3 pinned skills. If you archive a substance, its history is kept so you can bring it back later.
  • Daily check-ins. Your mood, and for each substance a craving score (0 to 10) and how much you cut back (0 to 100%). Also an optional written note (up to 500 characters) and the date.
  • If-then plans you save.
  • Reflections. What you write in Reflections, if you've allowed Reflections to use AI. See Reflections and our AI provider, Anthropic.
  • Feedback you send with Send feedback, saved with your account email so we can read it and follow up.

What the app creates as you use it

  • Progress. Banked days, money saved, badges and weekly averages are worked out from your check-ins. With each check-in we save your spending estimate at that time, so changing it later doesn't re-price days you've already saved.
  • Skill practice. Which skills you've practiced, how many times, and when you last did each one.
  • SOS. Only a count of how many times you've used SOS. The urge ratings you give during SOS stay on your phone and are never sent to us.
  • Reflections. The AI's replies, including each day's opening message and the closing message that gives your homework (a small experiment to try before the next session), the private notes Reflections writes for itself, a count of your sessions, and a tag on any message that matched our crisis-language check.
  • Account records. An internal account number, your sign-up date (we use it to work out your free trial), whether you've confirmed your email, when you agreed to the Terms of Use and this policy, when you allowed Reflections to use AI, and when you last used Start over. For security we also keep a counter that lets us sign out old sessions. We keep one-time codes and links only in scrambled (hashed) form. If we ever sent you a "you already have an account" email, we keep the time we sent it.

What your phone sends

  • Time zone. Your phone's time zone setting (for example, America/New_York). The app sends it when you sign up or log in, and again if it changes, so your day starts at 4am where you are. It shows your rough region. We don't collect your precise location.
  • IP address and app details. Like any server, ours sees your IP address and basic details about the app or browser making each request. See How we use your information and How long we keep information.

What Apple sends

Apple handles all payments. When you subscribe, buy Lifetime or restore a purchase, or when the app finds a purchase on your phone's Apple Account that our server doesn't know about yet, the app tells our server which plan it is and Apple's original transaction ID for the purchase. We store both with the Clearday account you're logged in to. We never see your card details or your Apple Account name or email. Apple also gives app makers standard sales and subscription reports, which don't include your name or email. If you've chosen in your iPhone settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may share crash reports and usage statistics with us in a form that doesn't identify you.

When you contact us

Anything you email to support@mentalhealthtools.net, including replies to our account emails, along with your email address.

What we don't collect: your phone number, contacts, precise location, Apple Health data, advertising identifier, or a push notification token.

What stays on your phone

Clearday keeps these things on your phone and never uploads them to us:

  • Your profile photo, if you add one. The app asks to use your camera or photo library only when you choose to add a photo. The photo is shrunk and saved on your phone. Clearday never uploads it to us or anyone else. It's removed when you tap Remove, log out, use Start over or delete your account.
  • An offline copy of your account data, including your email and check-in notes, so the app can open without a connection. It's removed when you log out, use Start over or delete your account. Because this copy is on your phone, please protect your phone with a passcode.
  • Your daily reminder, if you turn it on. Your phone schedules it itself, so we don't use a push notification service. Its title is "How was today?", with a short line about checking in, and it never names a substance. Logging out, Start over and deleting your account turn it off.
  • Your light or dark theme choice.
  • A short code made from your email address that remembers you've already seen the free-trial screen, so it doesn't show again when you log back in. It stays after you log out, and it's removed when you delete your account on that phone.

Like other app data, these can be included in your phone's own iCloud or computer backup, if you've turned that on. Deleting the Clearday app from your phone removes everything it keeps there. The app also uses standard phone features such as vibration, the keyboard and the status bar. These don't send anything to us.

Reflections and our AI provider, Anthropic

Reflections is a short daily AI conversation. Its replies are written by AI models made by Anthropic, PBC.

Nothing is shared without your permission

Whenever Reflections is off and you tap Start reflections (or Turn on) in Reflections, or you turn on "Use AI for Reflections" in You › Privacy & data, the app shows a permission screen that names Anthropic and lists what is sent. Nothing goes to Anthropic unless you tap Allow on that screen (it says Allow and start when you open it from Reflections). If you tap Not now, nothing is sent or saved, and Reflections stays off. Our server checks for your permission before every AI request and refuses to send one without it. Everything else in Clearday works either way.

When information is sent

  • Once a day, after you tap Start reflections, our server asks the AI to write the day's opening. This sends the information listed below, except your Reflections messages and the crisis-check result, before you've typed anything that day.
  • Each time you send a message, our server sends it along with the information below.
  • Reflections takes about 10 of your messages a day. Your last one also asks the AI for a closing message with your homework, and for a private note (see below).

Every request comes from our server, never from your phone. Requests don't include your email address, account number or IP address. They do include your first name, what you write in Reflections and excerpts from your check-in notes, so anything identifying in those goes along with them.

What Anthropic receives

  • What you write in Reflections, plus your recent Reflections conversations: up to the last 24 messages (yours and the AI's), which can include earlier days.
  • Your first name as it appears in the app.
  • The substances you're actively working on, with your banked days and this week's average craving and reduction for each, your total banked days and money saved, and whether you've checked in today.
  • The reasons you picked and the skills you pinned.
  • Short excerpts (up to 160 characters each) from up to 6 of your most recent check-in notes, with how many days ago each was written.
  • Your most recent homework.
  • Up to 14 of Reflections' private notes from earlier sessions, and how many sessions you've had.
  • Whether your message, or anything you wrote earlier that day, matched Clearday's check for crisis language.

Never sent to Anthropic: your email address, password, account number, IP address, time zone, profile photo, purchase or subscription details, if-then plans, skill practice history, feedback, SOS count, or your individual daily scores (the mood, craving and cut-back you log each day).

Reflections' private notes

At the end of each day's session, the AI writes itself a short private note (about 40 to 120 words) about what it learned: patterns, what helped and what didn't, your own words about why change matters, and sometimes people you mentioned. These notes let later sessions pick up where you left off. They're stored on our server and never shown in the app, and the newest 14 go to Anthropic with every Reflections request. The AI is told to stick to facts and your own words, with no diagnoses and nothing about suicide, self-harm or crisis. An AI writes them, so a note could include something it was told to leave out. You can ask us for a copy (see Your choices and rights). Start over or deleting your account erases them.

What you see in the app

The app shows today's session. Earlier messages stay on our server, up to the limits in How long we keep information, and your most recent 24 messages are sent as context in later sessions. You can ask us for a copy.

The automatic crisis-language check

Before a reply is written, our server automatically checks each Reflections message for words and phrases about suicide, self-harm or overdose. This is a computer check, not a person. If a message matches, the app shows crisis resources (call, text or chat with 988, an independent service not connected to Clearday), the message and its reply are tagged in our database, and the AI is told so it can respond with care. No one at Clearday is sent an alert, and we don't contact you, emergency services or anyone else. If one account sends an unusually large number of matching messages in one day, our server log records that account's number, never the messages (see How long we keep information).

AI training

We don't use your information to train AI models, and we don't sell or give it to anyone for that purpose. Anthropic's Commercial Terms don't allow it to train its models on what we send.

How Anthropic handles and keeps it

Anthropic handles this information for us as our service provider (a "processor") under its data processing agreement, which limits it to providing its service to us and keeping that service safe. Under Anthropic's published retention policy, it deletes what we send, and its replies, within 30 days. If its safety systems flag content, it may keep that content for up to 2 years and its safety scores for up to 7 years, or longer where the law requires. Deleting your Clearday account doesn't reach copies Anthropic already holds. Those expire on Anthropic's schedule.

Anthropic stores data in the United States, but it may process requests (run the AI) in other countries.

Turning it off

Turn off "Use AI for Reflections" in You › Privacy & data. It takes effect at once: nothing more is sent to Anthropic. Turning it off doesn't recall what Anthropic already received, and it keeps your past Reflections conversation and private notes on our server. To erase those, use Start over or delete your account. Start over leaves your permission setting as it is. If you turn Reflections back on, the permission screen shows you what's shared before anything is sent.

Cookies, tracking and Do Not Track

One sign-in cookie. Clearday uses one essential cookie to keep you logged in. It holds your internal account number and a security counter, nothing else. It lasts 30 days from your last use, and logging out removes it. Resetting your password logs you out on every device.

No tracking. Clearday has no other cookies and no analytics, advertising or tracking tools. We don't let any other company collect information about your activity in Clearday to follow you over time or across other apps and websites.

How we respond to Do Not Track signals (California)

Some browsers and devices can send a "Do Not Track" or Global Privacy Control signal. Clearday doesn't track you across other apps or websites, and it doesn't let any other company do so. Because there's no tracking to switch off, we treat everyone the same way whether or not a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal is sent.

How long we keep information

  • Your account, plan, check-ins, skill practice, if-then plans and feedback: until you delete your account, even if you stop using Clearday. Start over erases most of this (see Start over and Delete my account).
  • Reflections conversation: your newest 400 messages (yours and the AI's), plus your latest daily opening and homework. Older messages are removed automatically each time you send a new one.
  • Reflections' private notes: the newest 120. Older notes are removed automatically when a new one is saved.
  • Sign-ups that are never confirmed: deleted automatically about 7 days after the last confirmation email we sent.
  • One-time codes and links: stored only in scrambled (hashed) form. Codes stop working after 15 minutes, confirmation links after 24 hours, and password reset links after 1 hour.
  • IP addresses, account numbers and email addresses used to block abuse: only in our server's temporary memory, never in our database, and cleared when the server restarts.
  • Sign-in cookie: 30 days from your last use.
  • Server logs at our host: these record each request's IP address, the time, the web address requested, and information about the app or browser. For confirmation and password reset links, the web address includes the link's one-time token (the secret part of the link). Lines our own code writes to these logs never include what you write. One warning, written only if an account sends an unusually large number of Reflections messages that match the crisis-language check in one day, records that account's internal number. We clear these logs automatically at least every 30 days, so no entry stays longer than about a month.
  • Backups: we don't make backups or other copies of our database, and the app has no backup or export feature. Our host takes its own disaster-recovery copies of its servers, which can include our database, and keeps them for 7 days. Information you delete can stay in those copies for up to 7 days.
  • Anthropic: within 30 days, or longer for flagged content or where the law requires (see Reflections and our AI provider, Anthropic).
  • Resend: 30 days for the emails it delivers for us and their delivery records. If an email to you bounces or you mark it as spam, Resend may also keep your address on a do-not-send list until we remove it. We'll remove it if you ask.
  • Support emails: as long as we need them to help you, and no more than 2 years. We'll delete them sooner if you ask.
  • Apple keeps its own purchase records under Apple's privacy policy.
  • On your phone: the offline copy, photo and reminder stay until you log out, use Start over or delete your account on that phone. Everything Clearday keeps on your phone is removed when you delete the app.

Start over and Delete my account

Start over

Start over (You › Privacy & data › Start over) is a fresh start, not account deletion. It can't be undone.

It erases: your check-ins and notes, your substances and spending estimates, your reasons and day one, your skill practice and pinned skills, your if-then plans, your SOS count, and your Reflections conversation, private notes and session count. On the phone you use, it also clears the offline copy, your profile photo and your reminder. Another phone you're logged in on keeps its photo and reminder until you remove them there.

It keeps: your account (email, password, sign-up date and confirmation status, so the free trial doesn't restart), your first name, time zone, subscription and Apple transaction ID, any feedback you've sent, your Reflections permission setting (on or off), when you agreed to the Terms of Use and this policy, and when you last used Start over.

You can use it once per Clearday day (each day starts at 4am), and never twice within 6 hours.

Delete my account

Go to You › Privacy & data › Delete my account, enter your password, and tap Delete my account permanently. The same option is on the screen shown when a free trial ends. Deletion happens right away, with no waiting period, and can't be undone.

It removes your account and everything tied to it from our database: your account details, name, time zone, plan, check-ins, skill practice, if-then plans, Reflections conversation and private notes, feedback, subscription record and Reflections permission. On that phone it also clears the offline copy, your photo, your reminder and the free-trial code. If you're also logged in on another phone, it removes its offline copy and photo the next time it connects. To stop that phone's reminder, turn it off there or delete the app.

Deleted means erased. When you use Start over or delete your account, our database overwrites the deleted information in its file, instead of just marking the space as free.

  • Forgot your password? Use "Forgot password?" on the log-in screen first.
  • Can't log in at all? Email support@mentalhealthtools.net from your account's email address and we'll delete the account for you. If you never confirmed your email, the sign-up is deleted automatically after about 7 days.
  • Deleting your account doesn't cancel an Apple subscription. Apple keeps billing until you cancel. Cancel in your iPhone's Settings › your name › Subscriptions, or at apps.apple.com/account/subscriptions.

What deletion can't reach: copies already held by Anthropic and Resend (most expire on the schedules in How long we keep information; if your address is on Resend's do-not-send list, it stays there until we remove it, and we will if you ask), our host's logs and disaster-recovery copies (until they expire), Apple's purchase records, and support emails until we delete them (ask us and we will). Short-lived security entries in our server's memory are cleared when it restarts. Your theme choice stays on your phone until you delete the app.

Your choices and rights

Wherever you live in the United States, you can:

  • See your information. Ask us to confirm whether we hold information about you and send you a copy. This includes things the app doesn't show: older Reflections messages, Reflections' private notes and any crisis-language tags. We'll also give you a list of the companies that received your information.
  • Get a copy to take with you. We'll send your information in a common format you can save and reuse.
  • Correct it. Edit your name, plan, reasons, spending, substances and pinned skills in the app, and redo a check-in for any day in the past week (You › My quit plan › Log a past day). Email us to change your email address or fix anything else.
  • Delete it. Use Start over or Delete my account in the app, or email us. The app can't delete a single check-in or if-then plan on its own, so email us if you want one removed.
  • Take back your permission for Reflections at any time with the switch in You › Privacy & data (see "Turning it off" in Reflections and our AI provider, Anthropic).
  • Appeal if we turn down a request.

We don't sell your information, use it for targeted advertising, or use it to make automated decisions that seriously affect your life. If you'd still like to tell us never to sell your information, email support@mentalhealthtools.net with "Do not sell" in the subject. We'll record it and confirm within 60 days. We won't treat you differently for using any of these rights.

How to make a request

Email support@mentalhealthtools.net from the email address on your account. Putting "Privacy request" in the subject helps us find it, but we'll handle your request without it. You can also use the tools in the app described above. We'll need to confirm the request comes from you, usually by replying to the email address on your account. If you can't use that address any more, email us from another one and we'll find another way to confirm it's you. You don't need to create an account to make a request.

  • We'll answer within 45 days of receiving your request. If we need more time, we can take up to 45 more days, and we'll tell you why within the first 45 days.
  • Requests are free at least twice in any 12 months, and after that too unless they're clearly unfounded, excessive or repetitive. In that case we may charge a reasonable fee to cover our costs, or decline, and we'll explain why.
  • If we decline a request, we'll explain why and tell you how to appeal.

Appeals

If you disagree with our answer, reply to it with "Appeal" in the subject. We'll answer in writing within 45 days of receiving your appeal. If we deny your appeal, we'll tell you how to contact your state's attorney general.

How we protect your information

  • Connections between the app and our server are encrypted (HTTPS).
  • Passwords are stored only in scrambled (hashed) form that can't be turned back into the password. One-time codes and links are stored only in scrambled (hashed) form, and they expire.
  • Deleted information is overwritten in our database.
  • The database file can't be read by other accounts on our host.
  • The sign-in cookie is signed to protect it from tampering.
  • Repeated wrong passwords, codes and sign-ups are limited.
  • Our admin tools are password-protected, and only authorized people at Mental Health Tools LLC can use them.
  • Lines our own code writes to server logs never include what you write in Clearday.

No system is perfectly secure, and we can't promise that your information will never be accessed without permission. Please protect your phone with a passcode, because Clearday keeps an offline copy of your data there. Email to the address on your account is the main way we contact you, including about a security breach. If a breach affects your information, we'll tell you there.

If someone gave us your information without permission

If you believe someone gave us personal information without the legal ability or permission to do so, for example by signing up with your email address, contact us at support@mentalhealthtools.net and we'll delete it.
